**Claquete.ai** (formerly `kit-video-ia`; claquete is the clapperboard clapped at the start of every take) is the method behind the lessons, tutorials and social videos of ChatADV.<br/>
You pick the video type; Claude writes the script and the edit plan, records the screen, narrates, edits and reviews.<br/>
Did you record it yourself in Screen Studio? It edits your recording, assembles a version with the order of reasoning and pulls out the cuts for Reels.<br/>
You decide at two points, and guardrails refuse a script without an ok, narration without a review and a static screen.

### 🎞️ Animated screens (new in 2.1)

Every screen in a lesson can be a **React scene**: text that comes in word by word, a sphere of energy, aurora and
particles in the background, a chat being typed, a document writing itself, a number counting up, a camera moving
closer without cutting off anything. Claude writes the scene after your ok to the script, and the studio **photographs it
frame by frame with the page clock under control**, on the exact second of each word in the voice, in landscape and
vertical. It's the same engine as the ChatADV lessons.

- **React Bits components, on your machine.** The installer downloads 12 free components from [reactbits.dev](https://reactbits.dev)
  (SplitText, BlurText, ShinyText, GradientText, RotatingText, TextType, CountUp, AnimatedList, Orb,
  Aurora, Beams and Particles). With a **React Bits Pro** license, it also downloads the 6 the ChatADV lessons use
  (StaggeredText, AgenticBall, SpeedingText, AnimatedList, MagicTransform and Globe).
- **None of them ships in this repository.** The React Bits license (MIT + Commons Clause) does not allow redistributing the
  components: each one goes from the official registry straight to your machine, the way `npx shadcn add` would. The studio
  test refuses a React Bits component versioned here, and so does the pre-commit hook (in a fresh clone, turn it on with
  `git config core.hooksPath .githooks`).
- **Without them, it works the same:** every piece has its own version, made with motion alone.
- **Guardrails:** a piece cut in half at the edge of the camera, a zoom without focus, a scene with an error and a screen
  still for more than 2.5 s are refused at capture. `kit.py cenas <id> --previa` shows five shots of each scene before the full capture.

```json
{ "fala": "First, the context: say who you are and who the text is for.",
  "tela": { "tipo": "animada", "cena": "Cena2",
            "descricao": "A chat: the request is typed, the AI thinks and answers by writing." } }
```

The studio lesson `animada-exemplo` has three ready scenes so you can see how it's written.

> record a tutorial on how to register a client in my system, landscape and vertical, without showing real data

> make a 3-minute animated lesson on how to write a good collection e-mail

### 🎙️ Your Screen Studio recording, edited (new in 2.2)

You record the screen, the camera and the voice in [Screen Studio](https://screen.studio) 4, as always. Claude edits it without opening
the app and without an API, and delivers three versions of the same recording:

- **Edited:** out go the "like", the "you know", the repeated word, the sentence you got wrong and started over, the long pause and the
  wait for the tool (sped up and muted, with "▶▶ 4x" on screen). Spring zoom, the cursor and the clicks, your camera in the
  bubble, captions for what is heard and your brand's invitation at the end.
- **Hybrid:** your narration rebuilt into an order of reasoning (the problem, the solution, the uses, the demos, what
  comes next), with lesson scenes over your voice: every item comes in on the word you say. The screen comes out of Screen
  Studio itself: Claude records the cuts into a copy of the project, with the camera in the bubble, and you just click Export.
- **Vertical cuts:** each cut joins excerpts into a short narrative (hook, proof and invitation) of up to 90 s, at 1.3x, with
  the screen in a tighter zoom card, word-by-word captions and the camera at the top.

As in the lessons, you decide at two points, and guardrails hold back the rest:

- **Transcription on your computer:** the transcriber that comes inside Screen Studio 4 (Apple's speech, macOS 26 or
  newer) or the studio's local transcription. No API, no key.
- **Every splice is proven:** the studio transcribes 1.6 s before and 1.6 s after every cut and only accepts it if the neighboring
  words came out whole and the cut ones are gone.
- **Gate 1, the edit plan:** what goes out (every cut with the words around it), the hybrid order, the scenes and the
  vertical cuts. The video is only rendered with your ok.
- **Gate 2, the edited narration:** transcribed again and compared with the expected text; a subagent does the review.
- **Sensitive data:** the forbidden excerpt freezes the screen with your narration over it, and the mask covers the data. The copy for
  Screen Studio is refused if it would show what the studio hides.
- **Machine with little disk:** the render goes in parts of 900 frames and stops between one and the next if the disk gets tight.
- **Your project doesn't change:** Screen Studio is only read; the copy with the cuts is a new project, next to yours.

> edit my Screen Studio recording: take out the tics and the pauses, and make three cuts for Reels

> assemble my demo into an order of reasoning, problem, solution and what comes next, with scenes over my voice

## 🤖 How Claude works

Editing is not a video type: it's the way of working. Claude orchestrates the skills, your computer's
tools, the voice and the review on its own, and only stops at the two points where the decision is yours.

1. **Understands the request** and asks for what's missing: type, duration, audience, format.
2. **Script and edit plan, for you to approve.** The narration in each part and what shows up on screen. In the demo,
   also the path through the tool, the cuts, the speed-ups and the zooms. Nothing is recorded or narrated before your ok.
3. **Executes:** operates the computer, records, narrates with ElevenLabs, assembles and edits.
4. **Reviews with subagents:** narration, pronunciation and accent in each part, script, on-screen text, rhythm (nothing still
   for more than 2.5 s), zoom and sensitive data.
5. **Fixes** what failed and checks it again, without you asking.
6. **Delivers for your approval.** Once approved, the video goes to the folder you choose and the assembly is deleted.

<sub>The two gates are studio guardrails, not agreements: <code>narrar</code> refuses a script without an ok (or changed after the
ok) and <code>montar</code> refuses narration without a review. In the edited recording, the gates are the edit plan and the review of the edited narration. Recordings from other apps (Recordly, any video) arrive in 2.4.</sub>

## 🧑‍💻 Day to day

Open Claude in the studio folder and say **"I want to make a video"**. It asks for the type, the duration (in lessons, 2 to 5
minutes, your choice), the audience and the format, reads your brand and leads the rest.

- **You decide at two points:** the script with the edit plan, before recording and narrating; and the finished video, before
  publishing. In between, Claude works alone and fixes whatever the review flags.
- **The AI operates the computer.** In the demo it opens a browser of its own, clicks, types and records. Leave the machine
  free during the recording: don't touch the mouse in that window and close whatever is heavy.
- **Memory and disk.** Recording and rendering use a lot of memory, and the assembly of each video takes hundreds of MB until
  delivery. Keep about 10 GB free: `renderizar` refuses on its own with less than 5 GB, and `validar` and `plano` warn you.
- **Only the final video stays.** Once approved, `entregar` copies the video and the captions to your folder and deletes the assembly.
- **Brand material later:** say "set up my brand" and Claude reads the folder, the HTML or the `.md` you gave, fills in
  the brand and shows a sample in your visual style.

## 🧭 How it works

Everything comes from one file, `aulas/<id>.json`: the type, the duration you chose, the narration in each part and the screen that goes with it.

| Command | What it does | What it refuses |
|---|---|---|
| `novo <id> --tipo aula --minutos 3` | creates the file with the type and the duration | lesson, walkthrough or track without a duration of 2 to 5 min |
| `validar` | checks the file, for free | em-dash, forbidden word, long list, zoom without a valid focus, font outside Google Fonts; warns about a duration outside the target, a recording that doesn't exist yet and a disk with less than 5 GB free |
| `plano` | writes the script and the edit plan in a single document, with the path through the tool (`caminho`) and what will be recorded after the ok | · |
| `aprovar` | **gate 1**: registers the person's ok (the `de` of the recordings stays outside it) | file with an error |
| `narrar` | generates the voice of each part, with the timing of each word | script without an ok, or changed after the ok |
| `conferir` | **gate 2**: narration report for the review (with local transcription, if installed); `--aprovado` releases it | narration that changed after the report; a part the local transcription rejected |
| `cenas` | photographs the animated screens frame by frame, at the timing of the voice, in landscape and vertical (`--previa`: five shots of each one) | narration without a review; a scene that doesn't exist or has an error; a piece cut at the edge of the camera; a static screen |
| `montar` | works out when each element comes in and generates the Remotion project, always in both formats | narration without a review; a recording or image that doesn't exist; an animated screen not photographed or changed afterwards; **more than 2.5 s with nothing new**; a duration outside the type or your choice |
| `renderizar` | renders landscape (1920×1080) and/or vertical (1080×1920), according to `--formatos` (default `h`) | less than 5 GB free on the disk |
| `finalizar` | sound at -14 LUFS, `.vtt` and `.srt` captions, contact sheet | **static screen** measured in the finished video |
| `entregar <id> --destino <pasta>` | copies the final video, checks the copy and deletes the assembly | a copy whose size doesn't match |

`fazer <id>` runs everything in order and stops at the gates (code 2). `--formatos h`, `v` or `h,v` applies to `renderizar`,
`finalizar` and `fazer`. `finalizar --pagina` also generates the light versions (H.264, AV1 with `+faststart` and poster) to
put on a page, and runs again over a video that's already finished; with no render at all, it stops with "não há render" (there is no render).

### Your recording, command by command

The edited recording has a script of its own, `edicoes/<id>.json` (what goes out of the narration, the waits, the forbidden excerpts,
the masks, the zooms, the hybrid order, the scenes and the vertical cuts), and the commands of `scripts/gravacao.py`:

| Command | What it does | What it refuses |
|---|---|---|
| `novo <id> --projeto <pasta>` · `transcrever` · `texto` | reads the Screen Studio project and transcribes the narration word by word | a folder that isn't a Screen Studio 4 project; a paused recording |
| `plano` · `emendas` | the edit plan and the proof of each cut | an index outside the narration; a sped-up wait that crosses a forbidden excerpt |
| `aprovar` | **gate 1**: your ok to the plan | a plan that isn't the one from the current script |
| `audio` · `conferir` | the edited narration, transcribed again; `--aprovado` is **gate 2** | a cut without proof; a doubtful splice without acceptance; less than 85% similarity |
| `montar` · `hibrido` · `reels` | prepares the edited one, the hybrid one and the vertical cuts | narration in two blocks; a scene that comes in on a word you didn't say; an export that doesn't match the cuts; a vertical cut over 90 s |
| `fatias --nome` | records the cuts into a copy of the project, with the camera in the bubble, for you to export | without your ok; sensitive data that would show up; a copy that already exists |
| `renderizar --versao` | renders in parts of 900 frames (`--quadros` for a preview) | without the two gates; a script changed after preparing; less than 5 GB free |
| `entregar --destino` | copies the videos, the covers and the captions and deletes the assembly | a copy whose size doesn't match |

To prove that your studio's guardrails are standing: `python3 scripts/teste.py` (free, the voice is a tone generated on the
spot, and the Screen Studio recording is fake, with tones in place of the words). It tries to get past each gate
without the ok and fails if any of them lets it through.

### Ready screens

`capa` (cover), `lista` (list), `colunas` (columns), `fluxo` (flow), `frase` (quote), `numero` (number, counts up to the value), `imagem` (image), `video` (video) and, since 2.1,

```json

  "rotulo": "The ideas",
  "tela": { "tipo": "lista", "titulo": "A good request has *three parts*",
            "itens": [{ "texto": "Who you are", "quando": "who" },
                      { "texto": "An example", "quando": "example" }] } }
```

## 📏 The method, in five rules

1. **One idea per screen.** 20 to 40 words per part; the screen sums it up, it doesn't repeat the narration.
2. **Nothing still for more than 2.5 s.** Two guardrails measure it: one at the plan, another on the finished video.
3. **The voice is checked.** Wrong? Rewrite the sentence. The guardrail is never loosened.
4. **Zoom doesn't cut.** The focus comes fully into the light and the rest stays in the dark; check every zoom on the contact sheet.
5. **Real data never appears.** Test account, dummy data, and the recording discards itself if a text you marked as forbidden shows up. In your recording, the forbidden excerpt freezes the screen and the mask covers the data.
